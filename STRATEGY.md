## Sequence 03 · Market Analysis

### Market size

- **99.7%** of Swiss companies are SMEs. Almost 90% have fewer than ten employees.
- **281,000** businesses with 2 to 49 employees (2023): the range where need and budget meet.
- **333,000** one-person businesses. Large in number, mostly with small budgets.
- **103,000** companies in professional, scientific and technical services, the largest sector.
- **30,000** hospitality businesses, about 23,300 of them in gastronomy (2022), mostly small operations.
- **51,200** new companies registered in 2025. Q1 2026 up 4.9%, northwest Switzerland up 2.9%.

*Sources: SECO KMU portal, figures from BFS STATENT 2023; KMU-Studie 2024; Statista, gastronomy and hospitality counts 2022; statistik-journal.ch and startups.ch, 2026.*

### Price landscape

| Supplier | Typical price | What it usually covers |
|---|---|---|
| Templates, AI tools, Canva | Free to CHF 300 | Fast, polished, generic |
| Freelance designers | CHF 80 to 150 / hour | Logo and execution |
| Corporate design projects | CHF 1,900 to 10,300 | Average around CHF 5,100 |
| Agency concepts | From about CHF 10,000 | Analysis, strategy, implementation by a team |
| Jaymotion | CHF 3,900 to 8,500 | Strategy, identity, messaging and launch from one partner, with photo and film available |

*Sources: Gryps corporate design survey; Alchemy Zürich price guide 2025 and 2026.*

### Trends that matter

- **Polish is cheap, feeling is scarce** — AI makes professional-looking design available to everyone. What stands out now is a brand that feels specific and real. Commentary on the 2026 freelance market shows strategic, judgement-led work holding its value while pure execution loses it.
- **Concepts win in hospitality** — In Swiss gastronomy, concept-driven operators have grown their share among the largest players, while traditional businesses face pressure. Businesses that sell an experience need a brand that carries it.
- **Content has to feel real** — Audiences trust close, honest imagery more than staged corporate material. This favours a filmmaker who works cinematically but keeps things real.
- **Hiring pressure** — 82% of Swiss employers report difficulty filling positions. Employer branding is a growing need and a later opportunity for Jaymotion.

*Sources: We And The Color, May 2026; horizont.net foodservice ranking; ManpowerGroup Talent Shortage Survey via xantum.online, 2026.*

### Competitive landscape

| Alternative | Why businesses choose it | Where Jaymotion fits better |
|---|---|---|
| Doing nothing | No cost, the business runs | When a launch, growth step or new market makes the brand matter now |
| DIY with Canva or AI | Fast and cheap | When the brand has to feel specific to them |
| Freelance designer | Affordable, good execution | When strategy, words and imagery need to come with the design |
| Photographer or videographer | Easy to buy | When images should express a defined brand |
| Web or marketing agency | Website or campaigns in one project | When the brand should lead the website, not follow it |
| Branding agency | Full team, deep experience | When budget and scale call for one partner rather than a team |

> **What this tells us.** The opportunity sits between the freelancer and the agency, with businesses of 2 to 49 people that sell on experience and trust. Focus first on Basel and German-speaking Switzerland, where personal contact and on-site production are an advantage, and enter through the work these businesses already buy: photo, film and content.

## Sequence 04 · Audience Segmentation

The people Jaymotion works with are very good at what they do. They have loyal clients and want to keep them, win new ones and be recognised for their quality rather than their price. What they want is simple: a brand that looks, feels and is experienced the way their work is.

### How the segments build on each other

| Segment | Phases | What it is | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specialists | Phase 0 – Phase 3 (core throughout) | Knowledge, craft, hospitality | First features on RAW, first clients, then referrals into the local scene |
| Startups | Phase 1 – Phase 2 | Physical and digital products | Launch Kit and first brands; they grow into established businesses |
| Established | Phase 2 – Phase 3 | Owner-led businesses | Refresh and partnerships, reached through the portfolio |
| Agencies | Phase 1 – Phase 3 (parallel) | Partner channel | Bring clients from every segment |
| Later | Phase 3 | Employer branding | For established clients |

### Specialists — Phase 1

Businesses built on expertise, craft or hospitality. **Knowledge**: consultancies, coaches, trainers, advisors, online educators, B2B services. **Craft**: makers, ateliers, carpenters, roasters, bakeries, local producers. **Hospitality and gastronomy**: cafés, restaurants, bars, boutique hotels, concept gastronomy.

- **What drives them**: Pride in their work and loyal clients. They want to grow without losing what makes them special, and be chosen for quality rather than price.
- **Where we meet them**: Grown through word of mouth. The brand was made quickly at the start, by the owner, a friend or a template. Website and social media lag behind the quality of the work. Content happens when there is time.
- **Pain point**: New customers only feel the quality once they have experienced it. Until then the business competes on price or waits for referrals, and the brand looks different on every channel.
- **Trigger moments**: A new location or opening, a new menu, product line or service, an anniversary, a new competitor nearby, the step from referrals to active growth.
- **Leading questions**: How do people feel our quality before their first visit? How do we look as good as we are, in our own way? What should stay, and what should change?
- **Matching services**: Brand Identity & Story (mostly refresh), Photo and Film Production, Content Day, later Brand Partnership.
- **Where to find them**: The Basel scene in person, RAW features, LinkedIn, local trade and gastronomy associations, markets and coworking spaces.
- **Budget**: CHF 2,500 to 9,000
- **Reference project**: Brandműhely

### Startups — Phase 1

Young companies with physical or digital products or services: consumer goods, food and drink, apps, software, platforms, services. Launched or funded, preparing to go to market.

- **What drives them**: Speed and credibility. Being taken seriously by first customers, investors, partners and hires.
- **Where we meet them**: Product first. Name and logo improvised, often generated. Pitch deck, website, packaging or app each look slightly different. Everything is needed at once.
- **Pain point**: They look less established than their product is. Coordinating several freelancers costs time they don't have.
- **Trigger moments**: Launch, funding round, first hires, product release, trade fair, market entry.
- **Leading questions**: How do we look credible from day one? What is the one story customers, investors and hires should remember? How do we build a brand that grows with us?
- **Matching services**: Launch Kit, Brand Identity & Story (build), launch film, product photography.
- **Where to find them**: Startup programmes, incubators and coworking in Basel and Zurich, LinkedIn, funding and launch news.
- **Budget**: CHF 2,000 to 8,000
- **Reference project**: EU AI-readiness consultancy

### Established owner-led businesses — Phase 2

Businesses with five, ten or more years behind them and 10 to 49 employees, often family- or owner-led, with a strong local reputation.

- **What drives them**: Protecting their reputation, keeping loyal customers, attracting the next generation of customers and employees, preparing for succession.
- **Where we meet them**: The brand has grown piece by piece over the years, through many suppliers. The logo is old but loved. Materials don't match.
- **Pain point**: The brand no longer reflects who they are today and looks dated next to newer competitors. Younger customers and applicants don't connect. Changing it feels risky.
- **Trigger moments**: Generational handover, anniversary, expansion, a new website, a hiring push.
- **Leading questions**: What must stay so loyal customers still recognise us? What must change so new ones feel invited? How do we modernise without losing ourselves?
- **Matching services**: Brand Identity & Story (refresh), Photo and Film, Brand Partnership, later employer branding.
- **Where to find them**: Referrals from phase 1 clients, local business networks, trade associations, LinkedIn.
- **Budget**: CHF 5,000 to 15,000

### Agencies — Parallel, from phase 1

Web studios, performance and ad agencies, social media agencies of 2 to 15 people, usually built around one core service.

- **What drives them**: Results for their clients, client retention, growth without fixed headcount.
- **Where we meet them**: Clients arrive without a clear brand. The agency fixes it on the side or builds on a weak foundation.
- **Pain point**: A weak brand limits their ads, websites and campaigns. They have no brand or film specialist in-house, and hiring one doesn't pay for irregular demand.
- **Trigger moments**: Onboarding a client with an unclear brand, a campaign that needs content, a pitch that needs creative.
- **Leading questions**: How do we deliver better results without hiring? How do we offer brand and content without building a department?
- **Matching services**: Agency Partnership under a framework agreement.
- **Where to find them**: Direct approach between Basel and Zurich, Christoph's network.
- **Pricing**: CHF 1,200 per day or project prices

### Fit check for every enquiry

| Question | What the answer tells us |
|---|---|
| Does how they present themselves shape whether people buy? | Whether the brand is commercial for them, not decoration. |
| What is holding them back today? | The pain point and status quo we are solving. No clear pain, no project. |
| Is something coming with a date? | The reason to act now: a launch, offer, hire, funding round or new market. |
| Is there budget to act on it? | Orientation CHF 2,000 to 15,000 depending on segment. |
| Does one person decide? | Whether the project can move at the pace the process needs. |

*Not a fit for now: decisions by committee, no budget for presentation at all, or requests for a logo with no thinking behind it.*

A complete brand, built new or refreshed from what exists, and launched into use.

#### Brand Partnership — Phase 2

Your brand's creative director and content partner, quarter by quarter.

- **The moment**: Six months after launch, a café plans a new seasonal menu and an event. The owner doesn't want to start from zero or post something that feels off, and has nobody in-house who can plan and produce content that feels like the brand.
- **The problem**: After launch the brand is applied by many hands and slowly drifts. New moments need new content. Teams lack the confidence to create material that feels real and on brand, and an in-house creative doesn't pay for a small business.
- **They receive**:
  - A quarterly story session planning the next three months of moments
  - One production day per quarter: photos and short clips
  - Team coaching and a content playbook so staff create real, UGC-style content between shoots
  - A monthly brand check with fixes and new assets as needed
  - A direct line for brand decisions
- **What's different**: Most retainers sell either content volume or consulting hours. This combines planning, professional production and coaching the team to tell the story themselves.
- **The outcome**: The brand stays alive and consistent, content keeps flowing and the team feels confident using it.
- **Price**: CHF 1,200 to 1,800 per month, 6 month minimum, production included

## Sequence 06 · Process and Working Principles

### Three questions at the start of every project

1. What do people feel today when they meet the brand?
2. What should they feel?
3. What needs to change so they do?

### Four steps

1. **Analyse** — Intake form and material review before the first meeting. The three questions answered together, with a baseline of where the brand stands today.
2. **Propose** — A direction for message, look and feel, shaped with the client in a workshop until it feels right.
3. **Create** — Words, design and templates, with two structured feedback moments so the client is part of the work.
4. **Integrate** — The launch: a 90-day plan, the first pieces created together, a check-in after 30 days.

### Working principles

| Principle | In practice | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Prepared, not improvised | Intake form and material review before kickoff | The first meeting already goes deep |
| Direction before design | The three questions answered in writing before any visual work | Every design choice has a reason |
| Co-created | A direction workshop and two defined feedback moments | The client owns the result and uses it |
| Traceable decisions | Each choice in colour, type, image and word linked back to the brand direction | Clients can explain their brand themselves |
| Real over polished | Imagery and words that feel close and honest | Trust, and content that performs |
| Tested in real use | Identity checked on website, social, print and offer documents before delivery | It works in daily life, not only in a presentation |
| Clear scope and price | Written scope with what's in and out, fixed price, 50 percent at kickoff and 50 at launch | No surprises on either side |
| One contact, fast answers | One decision-maker on the client side, replies within one working day | Momentum and continuity |
| Focus | One core project at a time, booked start dates | Quality, and room for the day job |
| Done is defined | What "finished" means is agreed before starting | Protects against endless perfecting |
| Hours tracked | Every project measured against its estimate | Honest prices over time |

### Definition of success

| When | For the client | How it's measured |
|---|---|---|
| At launch | Owner and team explain the brand the same way | Three people asked separately give the same one-sentence answer |
| After 30 days | The brand is in use | Website, social profiles and offer documents updated |
| After 90 days | The client works independently | At least five new pieces created with the templates without Jaymotion |
| After 3 to 6 months | People notice | Compared with the baseline from the intake: enquiries, engagement, offer acceptance, customer feedback mentioning the brand |
| For Jaymotion | A project worth repeating | Hours within 20 percent of estimate, a written testimonial, one referral within six months |
